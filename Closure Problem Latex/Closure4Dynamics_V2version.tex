\begin{abstract}
    \textit{Draft Abstract:} This research investigates the "Closure Problem" in electromechanical energy harvesters, where high-dimensional analytical models ($n \approx 7$) must be reconciled with low-dimensional experimental observations ($m \approx 1$). Unlike traditional system identification, which assumes a correct model structure and tunes parameters, this work addresses \textbf{structural model error} ("missing physics") in the presence of \textbf{hidden dynamics}. We propose a cascade framework integrating Universal Differential Equations (UDEs), Port-Hamiltonian Neural ODEs, and Koopman Operator theory to reconstruct hidden states and learn missing physical terms while strictly enforcing thermodynamic constraints.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: The Reactivity and Closure Problem}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The System Context}
Electromechanical energy harvesters represent a class of "reactive" multi-domain dynamical systems. The system state $\state(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ evolves according to coupled mechanical and electrical laws, driven by inputs $\inputvec(t)$ (e.g., vibration source). However, the physical realization of the device offers only a limited observation $\outputvec(t) \in \mathbb{R}^m$, where $m \ll n$. 

Typically, only voltage or tip displacement is measurable, leaving internal states (such as coupled modes, magnetic hysteresis states, or thermal gradients) hidden.

\subsection{The Closure Problem}
Standard engineering approaches assume the analytical model $f_{analytical}$ is structurally correct and only requires parameter estimation ($\theta$). However, experimental evidence suggests the presence of \textbf{missing physics}—dynamics not captured by the derived constitutive equations. 

The research problem is thus defined as a "Closure Problem": How do we learn a correction term $g_{learn}(\state, \inputvec)$ such that:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\state} = f_{analytical}(\state, \inputvec; \theta) + g_{learn}(\state, \inputvec)
\end{equation}
subject to the constraint that $\state$ is only partially observable via $\outputvec = h(\state)$.

\subsection{Kalman Filtering Variants (EKF/UKF)}
State estimation techniques like the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) utilize a predictor-corrector architecture to estimate hidden states $\hat{\state}$.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strength:} Well-suited for noise reduction when the model structure $f_{analytical}$ is accurate.
    \item \textbf{Limitation:} In the presence of structural error (missing physics), the Kalman Gain often "smears" the error into the state estimate, leading to bias rather than identifying the missing dynamic term.
\end{itemize}

\section{SciML Frontiers: Investigated Avenues}

\subsection{Universal Differential Equations (UDEs) \& Adjoint Optimization}
To address the "missing physics," we investigate UDEs where a Neural Network (NN) approximates the unknown term $g_{learn}$. 
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\state} = f_{physics} + \text{NN}(\state)
\end{equation}
\textbf{The Partial Observability Challenge:} Standard UDE training requires full state data. We propose utilizing \textbf{Adjoint Sensitivity Analysis} to backpropagate the error from the output trajectory $\outputvec(t)$ through the ODE solver to update the NN weights, effectively "inferring" the influence of hidden states on the output.

\subsection{Latent Dynamics \& Koopman Operator (HAVOK)}
Since the true dimension of the "active" physics is unknown, we employ Hankel Alternative View of Koopman (HAVOK) analysis. By constructing Time-Delay Embeddings from the single output $\outputvec(t)$, we can reconstruct a diffeomorphic shadow manifold of the full system.
\begin{itemize}
    \item This validates if the "7-state" model is necessary or if the dynamics live on a lower-dimensional attractor.
    \item Allows for linear embeddings of nonlinear phenomena.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Port-Hamiltonian Neural ODEs (PH-NODE)}
A critical flaw in standard Black-Box Neural ODEs is the violation of conservation laws (e.g., generating free energy). For an energy harvester, this is physically inadmissible.

We propose a \textbf{Port-Hamiltonian framework} where the Neural Network learns the Hamiltonian (Energy function) $\mathcal{H}(\state)$ and the Damping matrix $R(\state)$, rather than the vector field directly:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\state} = [J(\state) - R(\state)] \nabla \mathcal{H}(\state) + G(\state)\inputvec
\end{equation}
This guarantees stability and passivity by construction, effectively constraining the "search space" for the missing physics to only physically realizable models.

\subsection{Multi-Fidelity Transfer Learning}
To overcome the lack of hidden state data, we propose a \textbf{Sim-to-Real Transfer} approach:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pre-training:} Train the UDE structure on high-fidelity FEM simulation data (where all $\state$ are known).
    \item \textbf{Transfer:} Freeze the learned structure of the hidden couplings.
    \item \textbf{Fine-tuning:} Use experimental output data $\outputvec$ to tune only the coefficients of the missing physics term via Adjoint Optimization.
\end{enumerate}
